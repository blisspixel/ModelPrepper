# Intent

ModelPrepper exists to make individual custody of useful AI models ordinary.

People should be able to preserve published models on their own media, run them privately, study them, and retain capabilities without depending on a provider's continued approval.

## The principle

The shorthand is a Second Amendment absolutist's prepper mindset applied to AI: distribute capability, distrust centralized permission, and keep practical reserves. This is a political analogy, not a claim about equivalent legal protections.

Freedom of expression, independent research, and access to published knowledge are the project's values. Corporate accounts and government-approved programs should not become the only practical way to use capable AI.

Preservation does not require predicting a ban. Companies close, repositories vanish, hosts change ownership, and useful releases become inconvenient to obtain. A verified local revision survives the loss of its download button.

## Why the cyber debate matters

Anthropic's September 29, 2026 [GLM-5.3 article](https://www.anthropic.com/research/glm-5-3-and-the-spread-of-advanced-cyber-capabilities) reports advanced exploitation capabilities and removable safeguards. It recommends government evaluations and treats restrictions on access and modification as advantages of closed distribution.

The project's concern is concentration of power. A provider able to monitor, authorize, and withdraw access also controls legitimate research and use. Commercial incentives deserve scrutiny when sellers of controlled access advocate policies affecting alternatives.

A reported valuation or claim about hidden motives is unnecessary to this argument. Policies can concentrate control even when proponents sincerely intend to reduce harm.

Anthropic's July 27 [stated position](https://www.anthropic.com/news/position-open-weights-models) rejects a blanket ban and supports mandatory testing of sufficiently capable models, open and closed. These sources do not prove a secret category-ban agenda. Our concern is that requirements predicated on permanent control, revocation, or monitoring could make open distribution impractical. That is the project's interpretation of the risk.

ModelPrepper rejects that concentration and preserves individual options. Its purpose extends across companies, nationalities, and model families.

## The utility

People do not need to adopt this politics. They should be able to say "keep these things" and receive a dependable archive.

Models are the first supported artifact. Large files, multiple representations, licenses, and runtime dependencies make them a demanding starting point. Later adapters may preserve selected datasets or software releases through the same contracts. The first release remains focused on models.

## What done means

A non-specialist installs one native Rust binary on Linux, macOS, or Windows, initializes a vault, selects models, and preserves copies within a budget.

An unattended run survives interruption without corrupting a completed revision, overspending reservations, or silently following a moving branch.

A person can unplug a full disk and still see its inventory. Manifests rebuild a lost catalog. A verified replica restores files after upstream disappears.

A revision has a documented loading procedure and an honest offline status. Keeping bytes and proving they run are separate achievements.

## Non-negotiable properties

- User custody: no required hosted control plane, telemetry, or remote kill switch.
- One native binary: no required language runtime, container, inference server, or external downloader.
- Preservation: upstream changes never overwrite or delete sealed revisions.
- Transparency: inspectable plans show bytes, source identity, rights evidence, and reasons.
- User direction: explicit selections first, optional discovery second.
- Durability: ordinary files and versioned manifests outlive the program.
- Responsibility: authorized access and upstream license conditions remain applicable.

[Operating responsibilities](docs/notice.md) apply. Engineering defaults protect the archive from damage; they do not make the utility an arbiter of acceptable opinions.
